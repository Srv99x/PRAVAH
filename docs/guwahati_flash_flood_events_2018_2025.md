# Verified Guwahati flash-/urban-flood events, 2018–2025

**Research cut-off:** 18 September 2026  
**Scope:** rapid pluvial/urban flooding or flash flooding in Guwahati/Kamrup Metropolitan. Prolonged rural Brahmaputra inundation was excluded unless the source also documented a discrete city event.  
**Important limitation:** this is a **source-verified inventory, not a claim that every rain spell was captured**. Public reports frequently omit either a gauge total, event time, or street-level location. Rows retain “not reported” rather than substituting modelled or nearby rainfall.

## Why 2018–2021 and 2023 have no retained row

Flooding certainly occurred in Assam and Guwahati during those years, but this review did not locate an event source that simultaneously met the minimum inventory requirements: **an exact Guwahati event date, a named geolocatable locality, a rapid urban/flash-flood effect, and a traceable official or established-news URL**. Many accessible reports were statewide river-flood summaries, generic “parts of Guwahati” photo reports, social posts, or retrospective studies without an exact event timestamp. Rather than manufacture precision, those candidate events were excluded.

This is an explicit **verification gap**, not evidence that Guwahati had no urban floods in those years. A complete historical census would require ASDMA/Kamrup Metro situation reports, GMC pump logs/complaint records, traffic-police incident logs and raw IMD hourly station data that are not exposed in a consistent public archive.

## Validation suitability

### Best available rows

- **5 Aug 2024:** best for a citywide binary/ordinal flood footprint because the rainfall window, one-hour trigger, many named localities and knee-depth description align.
- **10 May 2025:** useful for a short-duration onset test, but Amingaon rainfall is not spatially representative of the city centre.
- **29 May 2025:** useful for validating severe impact in Rukminigaon/Bishnujyoti Path because rescue boats and knee-depth flooding are documented.
- **14 Jun and 22 Jul 2025:** valuable low-total/high-impact cases for testing drainage sensitivity and antecedent blockage/saturation effects.

### Not sufficient by themselves for pixel-/street-level supervised labels

No retained article provides all of: point coordinates, onset to the minute, maximum surveyed depth, recession time, velocity, flood polygon and station-matched sub-hourly rainfall. “Knee-deep” is an ordinal observation, not a calibrated depth. The 30 May 2025 rainfall reports also use unreconciled windows/stations.

For model validation, treat the inventory as **event discovery and weak labels**. Confirm each label with CCTV/video timestamp, municipal pump/road-closure logs, geolocated photographs, citizen calls, and raw IMD/AWS/radar accumulation before calculating skill scores.

## Drainage-system note

The official ASDMA Guwahati flood compendium identifies the **Bharalu–Bahini river system** and **Basistha River** among the city's natural drainage channels and says the Bharalu and Basistha stretches are chronically flooded. [ASDMA, *Urban Floods in Guwahati*](https://asdma.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/Guwahati%20floods.pdf)

The table's basin labels are therefore deliberately broad. A neighbourhood name alone does not prove which outfall or drain reach carried a particular flood pulse; detailed catchment GIS or field tracing is still required.
