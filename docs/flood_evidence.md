# Flood evidence: does PRAVAH prioritise verified urban-flood localities?

Generated by `app/flood_evidence.py`. Nothing was tuned to improve any number below.

## What this is, and what it is not

- **The labels are weak.** Each affected place is a **locality centroid**: one point from OpenStreetMap for a named neighbourhood, road or bus stop, assigned to the 1 km grid cell that contains it. They are **not surveyed flood polygons**. A 1 km cell around a point may or may not have flooded, and a flooded street can lie in the next cell.
- **None of these events was used to fit or select anything** in the trigger model, the labels, the susceptibility classes, the priority matrix, the review level or the flood-class thresholds. The flood-event ledger did not exist when those were fixed.
- **The ledger is a source-verified inventory, not a census** (`docs/guwahati_flash_flood_events_2018_2025.md`). The 14 Jun 2022 Boragaon row is excluded from the flood ledger (its source grades it C for flood validation, B for landslide). Years 2018-2021 and 2023 have no retained row, so this is 7 events in 2024-2025 only.
- **Not every named place could be located.** 40 of 58 event-locality rows have coordinates; the rest have no OpenStreetMap feature with exactly that name and were left blank, not guessed (`data/raw/urban_flood_events.csv`, `geocode_quality`). Unresolved localities are absent from every count below, which biases the test towards the places OpenStreetMap happens to name.

## How priority is computed here

Same pipeline as the app on `main`. The daily-peak trigger comes from `trigger_prob_daily.parquet` (RandomForest on ERA5-Land, trained on landslide labels, about 9 km resolution, 19 weather points). Priority is the 4x4 decision matrix in `app/config.py`.

- **Flood layer ignored (iv):** priority = matrix[landslide class][trigger tier]. This is exactly what `main` produces today.
- **With the flood layer:** priority = the higher of that and matrix[flood class][trigger tier], using the same matrix and the rows Low / Moderate / High by name. The combination rule (maximum of the two) is my choice; it was not tuned.
- **Review priority** means level 3 (Elevated) or above, the level selected for the landslide-only system. It was not re-selected for the flood layer.
- **Rank** is among all 904 cells that day, tie-honest: `a-b` means at best `a`th and at worst `b`th, because cells at the same priority are not ordered. Cells with no DEM rank last.

**The flood class is not selective.** With the specified rule it gives High to 570 cells, Moderate to 0, Low to 232 and null to 102 (see `docs/flood_layer_sensitivity.md`). Being in flood-High is therefore close to the base rate for any valley-floor cell, and the last columns below show what chance would give.

## Reading the result plainly

- **Pooled over the 7 events**, 4 of 38 affected cells reached review priority with the flood layer ignored (3.0 expected if the same number of cells were picked at random each day; observed/expected = 1.3), and 8 of 38 with the flood layer (6.8 expected; observed/expected = 1.2).
- **On this evidence PRAVAH does not show that it prioritises the verified flood localities better than chance.** The flood layer raises the count because it puts more cells into the queue, not because it picks the affected cells out of it.
- **5 Aug 2024 (78.4 mm, the strongest single event):** 2 of 11 affected cells reached review with the flood layer ignored and 3 of 11 with it (chance 0.20 and 0.34). 1 of 11 stayed at Routine with the flood layer against 7 without; but 7 sit at Watch, below review. The affected cells' triggers were: 8 cells at T2 (0.37 to 0.48); 3 cells at T3 (0.71 to 0.72), although the source reports the city's highest daily rain of the season up to that point. **This event is not a success story and should not be presented as one.**
- **Zero affected cells reached review** on 4 of 7 events (2025-05-10, 2025-05-29, 2025-06-14, 2025-07-22). On all of them the model's highest trigger anywhere in the district was below 0.5, so no layer stacked on top of it could flag anything. That is consistent with ERA5-Land at about 9 km not resolving short, local downpours (an inference; not tested here). The inventory singles out 14 Jun and 22 Jul 2025 as low-total, high-impact cases for testing drainage sensitivity; HAND contains no drains.
- **The flood class is close to uninformative here.** 71% of the cells with a HAND are flood-High, and 33 of 38 (87%) affected cells are High, about the base rate. The specified rule cannot separate flooded localities from the rest of the valley floor.
- **The flood layer changed the priority of 12 of 38 affected cells and moved 4 of them into review**, but it did so for many other cells too (next section).
- **Not tested here:** whether the model scores flood-affected cells higher than unaffected cells on the same day at finer resolution, anything about depth or duration, and any event before 2024 (the ledger has none).

### Side effect on the review queue, replay 2018-2025

The review level was chosen for the landslide-only system (median queue of at most 30 cells per alert day). Adding the flood layer changes the workload:

| | Flood layer ignored | With flood layer |
|---|---|---|
| Alert days (of 2922) | 519 | 523 |
| Flagged cells per alert day, median (IQR) | 28 (7 to 84) | 66 (11 to 183) |
| Largest single-day queue | 460 | 755 |
| Cells flagged at least once | 460 | 755 |

With the flood layer the median queue is 66 cells, above the 30-cell limit the review level was selected under (`docs/priority_matrix.md`); the review level has not been re-selected for the combined layer.
